Methodology

Four schools. One instrument. No mixing.

Each rule set below is kept deliberately separate; never blended into a single paragraph; because a live calculator session only ever draws from one. These are simplified rule sets prepared for this project; they are not fatwas. Unresolved or borderline cases should go to a qualified scholar.

Hanafi

The user must be at or above nisab at the beginning and end of the lunar year; a mid-year dip below nisab does not reset the zakat year on its own.

Gold & silver: bars, coins, worn jewelry, unused jewelry, and jewelry held as investment are all included. Only the metal's value counts, not stones or gems.

Debts: subtract debts that are due immediately, overdue, or expected within the next 12 months. Future interest, not-yet-due bills, and the full balance of a long-term mortgage or student loan are not subtracted.

Receivables: included if repayment is expected. Doubtful or unlikely repayments are flagged, and the calculation may be delayed until the money is actually received.

Maliki

Wealth must remain at or above nisab for one complete lunar year, continuously. If it falls below, the zakat start date resets; a new year begins once nisab is reached again.

Gold & silver: bullion, coins, jewelry stored as wealth, investment jewelry, and excess jewelry not reasonably intended for personal use are included. Customary personal jewelry, and diamonds/gemstones (unless business inventory), are excluded.

Debts: may reduce cash, gold/silver, investments, and business inventory; but only debts currently due or expected within 12 months.

Receivables: an ordinary personal loan owed to the user is not zakated annually while unpaid; one year of zakat is calculated once received. Unpaid business sales or invoices are included if repayment is reasonably expected.

Shafi’i

Wealth must remain at or above nisab for one complete lunar year, continuously; a dip resets the year, which restarts once nisab is reached again.

Gold & silver: bullion, coins, savings gold/silver, investment or resale jewelry, and business jewelry inventory are included. Customary personal jewelry and diamonds/gemstones (unless business inventory) are excluded.

Debts: personal debts; credit cards, student loans, mortgage payments, personal loans, and current bills; are not subtracted from zakatable assets.

Receivables: if repayment is likely, the amount is included as a zakatable asset every year, without waiting for physical receipt. Doubtful repayment is flagged as tentative.

Hanbali

Wealth must remain at or above nisab for one complete lunar year, continuously; falling below at any point resets the year.

Gold & silver: bullion, bars, coins, savings/hoarded gold or silver, and jewelry held for trade are included, along with excessive, non-customary, or impermissible jewelry. Customary jewelry genuinely used for permissible adornment is excluded. Borderline cases are marked Scholar Review Required.

Stocks/shares: not treated as trade inventory and not included directly. If sold and the proceeds remain as cash for a full hawl while at or above nisab, the cash is zakated; not the stock itself.

Debts: the full outstanding balance owed to creditors is subtracted, whether or not repayment has been requested, with no 12-month limit; this includes long-term mortgage balances.

Receivables: money lent to others is excluded entirely from the calculation while it remains unpaid. It is not treated as tentative under this rule set; simply not counted until repaid.

These are simplified rule sets prepared for this project as a community and educational exercise. They are not formal fatwas or authoritative statements of any madhhab. Specialists within a school may identify details beyond this scope. Unresolved cases should be directed to a qualified scholar.
